# First-hit tuning: cover the right programs before predicting their latency

**Decision.** Keep the existing first-hit tuner and winner cache. On the weak **flat router**, test two mechanisms before training anything bigger: a geometry-driven forward **and** backward column-ownership opener, and retrieval of two distinct, compatible, previously measured schedules. Both fresh flat router winners came from **retrieval**, not the learned ranker. Leave the packed-router, expert and shared-MLP openings alone by default. Introduce learned ranking only where it beats both cheap mechanisms at the same real effort, including cold time. The reusable product is a way for an adapter to supply regions of legal schedules and features, not a table of optimal tiles for named GPUs.

This is a CPU-only review of the [study and contracts](mxfp8_moe_heuristics_20261120.md) and its [durable archive](../autotune_reports/mxfp8_moe_heuristics_20261120/ARCHIVE.md). No new GPU measurements, fitting of a deployment artifact, changes to kernels or defaults. Terms below: *incumbent* is the initial legal plan; *regret* is selected time / best **observed** eligible time − 1; *first hit* includes the work to obtain a validated winner on a cache miss. These are three different outcomes: time to find a decent schedule, steady-state speed with that schedule, and speed versus BF16.

## What the data actually distinguish

The original 31 completed large contexts (24 discovery, seven holdout) supply 3,038 observations. Each invokes affinity, a router, experts and a shared MLP, i.e. 124 invocations, **not 124 independent shapes**. Below, geometries use the analyzer's semantic grouping across boards. Regret is per invocation from its actual eight-trial prefix against the best of its 32 trials; the affinity has just two alternatives.

| Component | Invocations / semantic geometries | Eligible / observed | Median / worst 8-trial regret | Priority |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Flat training router | 7 / 7 | 224 / 224 | 76.48% / 81.63% | Change early region coverage |
| Packed training router | 24 / 18 | 768 / 768 | 0.21% / 1.50% | Protect the opening; improve implementation for large wins |
| Routed experts | 31 / 31 | 991 / 992 | 0.17% / 3.74% | Target tails, missing families, cold time |
| Shared recomputed MLP | 31 / 11 | 989 / 992 | 0.17% / 0.76% | Protect the opening; do not generalize to all MLPs |
| Affinity | 31 / 16 | 62 / 62 | — | Measure both cheaply; no case for a learned selector |

**The identifiable flat miss is forward coverage.** Inspecting configs in [observations.parquet](../autotune_reports/mxfp8_moe_heuristics_20261120/analysis-final/observations.parquet): all 56 opening attempts kept `forward.column_groups=4`. Every 32-trial winner used **one column per CTA in both directions**; QB backend, threads, row grouping and metadata were not universal. The first such joint candidate appeared at attempt 13 or 14. The first candidate within 2% of the local 32-trial oracle appeared at attempts 11–31 (median 30). This is not an absent kernel: [the current flat opener](../rtx/autotune/routing.py) already contrasts **backward** column geometry at fixed forward plan. More lanes/column and more column CTAs can help a long underfilled reduction, while grouping adjacent columns can improve row-major access. Try both; don't force one.

Post hoc support check, not an out-of-sample result: keep the first seven (or six) ordinary proposals, use the first one (or two) chronologically logged candidates with both column groups one in the remaining slot(s). Result: median/worst regret **39.65/45.05%** with one, **12.19/36.11%** with two. This uses future *candidate identities* from the same adaptive logs; the test does not show a generator would produce those plans cold. The region matters; which feasible plan within it matters too. Exact one-field pairs support a backward effect (groups 8 / 1 time ratio 1.272 median, seven contexts). They do not isolate both directions as the sole cause of the prospective improvement. In particular Torch QB / native-column QB is 0.832 median in seven flat contexts but 1.721 in 24 packed ones. Never transfer “Torch QB is faster” between the two.

**Which mechanism won when actually run?** In the [E56 journals](../autotune_reports/mxfp8_moe_heuristics_20261120/prospective-gpu0/d384_e56_k4_h320_s256_b8_t18432_flat/prior/tuning/routing_effort/) and [E48 journals](../autotune_reports/mxfp8_moe_heuristics_20261120/prospective-flat-transfer-gpu1/d512_e48_k4_h448_s384_b8_t9728_flat/prior/tuning/routing_effort/), both prior searches tried the same two compatible retrieval proposals at attempts 4 and 5. E48 chose the first (native-warp QB, CuTe metadata); E56 chose the second (Torch QB/metadata). Both have one column per CTA forward/backward. Attempt 7 was the actual `frozen_ranker` proposal; it lost on both. The measured results:

| Fresh flat case | Router F+B, ordinary → prior | Whole F+B, ordinary → prior | Whole ratio | Same actual IDs/inverse on tested input? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| T18432/D384/E56/K4 | 3.329 → 2.141 ms | 5.053 → 3.806 ms | 1.328× | No |
| T9728/D512/E48/K4 | 1.895 → 1.106 ms | 3.570 → 2.777 ms | 1.285× | Yes |

These are **ordinary eight vs prior eight**, not BF16 comparisons or a clean ablation of only retrieval: all three tuned components received the policy. Across the three fresh packed pairs, whole-time changes were −1.05%, +0.31%, +0.46%; separate-process arms don't resolve sub-percent claims. For E56 the sum of the three reported component *search* elapsed times grew 51.5→61.3 s; E48 59.9→66.5 s. Illustratively that additional search time alone would require ~7,900 and ~8,300 calls, respectively, to amortize at the observed whole-step gains. This is **not** measured total startup or a user break-even guarantee: outer compile/setup, cache state, variance and actual reuse count matter. Log invocation-to-ready wall time separately. The main 32-trial study achieved ~1.06× median whole speedup vs the matched aggressively compiled compact-checkpoint BF16, with losses on some cases.

**No model can select an unimplemented speedup.** Three coupled 96-trial expert geometry/transport/depth/store panels found only 0.52–0.73% more expert speed in those panels; measured whole improvement on one anchor was 0.44%, within the visit spread. This is evidence against another broad tile sweep there, not proof of a global optimum. On the packed E128/K4 anchor ordinary standalone QB/repair forward was 1.084 ms vs router F+B 1.480 ms; Sinkhorn F/B 0.104/0.108 ms; metadata F+B 0.043 ms. The node trace recorded five repair rounds, ~514 µs across 11 top-k kernels versus 16.8 µs across 23 D2D copies (12.29 MB). For a substantially faster *well-tuned* packed path investigate reducing repeated selection/sorting/round work while preserving policy. For experts investigate joint recompute/operand prep and reducing actual FP8 gather/combine traffic, not just tile order. Routing scores/metadata move; dispatch also physically moves feature payloads. Traces perturb timing, standalone scopes overlap; none of these numbers is an exact in-layer share.

## What the existing models know, and what they do not

- **Healthy positives, few independent decisions.** Every main exported status was OK; four timings ineligible. Thus none of the five frozen bundles has a fitted feasibility head. This cannot train a failure classifier or establish 100% deployment success: static rejection, incomplete contexts and aborted jobs are outside this completed-case view. Preserve the original failed tiny control (264 workspace vs 256 actual scheduled rows) and fixed-source recovery as separate evidence. Treat compile error, numerical failure, unstable timing, timeout/censoring, fatal device failure and statically illegal proposals as distinct events. No invented latency for any. Record attempted wall cost even on failure; a device fault stops collection.
- **Offline metrics rank a different decision.** [Grouped evaluation](../benchmarks/analyze_moe_heuristics.py) protects one incumbent and chooses seven *already measured* configurations; discovery uses grouped out-of-fold predictions, not a random row split. Flat discovery has six geometries; its only main flat holdout has ordinary/random-median/shallow-tree/ExtraTrees regret 41.32/18.66/9.83/0%. Excellent retained-candidate recall does not show that ExtraTrees will *propose* those candidates. Five fresh prior cases tested a different, portable hybrid search. Two shared holdouts reuse discovery component geometries. The offline tree objective assigns ineligible rows `log(100)`; this makes an unstable success appear catastrophically slow. Use eligible timings only for speed; keep such attempts in the budget and as evidence of instability. Equal-weight geometries and repeated inputs separately from config counts. Reserve unseen length distributions, not just renamed whole-MoEs.
- **Verify train/serve parity at the vector and ID levels.** [portable_features](../rtx/autotune/pretrained.py) builds the `offline.*` ratios at runtime too via [prior_features](../rtx/autotune/prior.py); their names are not evidence of leakage. CPU audit: reapplied the runtime transform to all 3,038 raw journal feature maps and the documented prepared-FP8 gather canonicalization to compare with the Parquet export. The only remaining delta was an offline-added `context.device.sku.sku_family=geforce_rtx_5090` on all 2,046 non-shared rows (`_reconcile_device_aliases`); use that for split auditing, not as a predictor input. Independently rebuilt present flat/packed router and expert adapters from two GPU0 prospective session contexts, no GPU execution: all 32 candidate vectors matched their raw served vectors. In **all 3,038** exported vectors `offline.analytical_baseline_ms` is exactly the **1e-6 ms fallback**: no usable roofline anchor in this dataset. This does not invalidate context-centered ranking; it means an absolute residual model cannot get physical scale from that field here.
- **Config migration matters.** 17/24 expert donor-winner payloads in the frozen artifact still say `gather_mode=warp` on the proved inactive path. `FrozenPrior.retrieval()` deserializes/normalizes these for the target adapter; `FrozenPrior.order()` instead matches the stored ID directly against target pool positions for its retrieval stage. A proved alias can thus lose a retrieval match in the latter path; the raw IDs must remain as provenance. Use the normalized target ID for matching *after* contract validation, deduplicate both features and training weights by the active schedule, and keep original IDs for replay. This does **not** explain the two flat wins. Validate that a full-Python source change is compatible with a portable prior by an explicit semantic/config version; stale emitted programs must not share durable runtime winner keys.
- **Avoid fitting an absolute clock to a rank problem.** [ContextRankingModel](../rtx/autotune/pretrained.py) fits ratios to a context median (pointwise centered regression, not pairwise training). [FrozenPrior](../rtx/autotune/prior.py) already has retrieval, rank, feasibility and preparation-cost slots; uncertainty returned by the ranking head is not a calibrated probability. The old three-device [linear/MLP study](prior_prospective_results_20260913.md) is instructive, not a transfer benchmark for these MoEs: eight-trial MXFP8 backward retrieval/ranker mean regret 2.09/59.30%, while fused forward 21.65/12.48%. Today's strong shared opening can also beat yesterday's weak ordered baseline without any ML. No general default follows from the winning average across families.

## A small, composable selector

One public `AutotunePolicy`, one [orchestrator](../rtx/autotune/orchestrator.py), no MoE-specific inference engine:

1. **Legality and identity.** Adapter owns `rejection`, canonical serialization, independent correctness evaluator and the incumbent. Filter only proven hardware/implementation/semantic restrictions; fold aliases only with tests against the *raw emitted launch plans*. Timing similarity is not alias proof. Keep strict forward/training, precision, scaling, routing independence, measurement and gradient contracts. An exact compatible cached validated winner bypasses ranking; compatible neighbor configs are proposals, not transferred timings. Do not hash diagnostic counters into winner identities.
2. **Structural coverage before scoring.** Let an adapter optionally supply `region(config, context)`, a bounded generator of complete canonical representatives and cheap soft priorities with reasons. Generic [mixed_pool](../rtx/autotune/sampling.py) and the progressive effort engine deduplicate and reserve space **before** local mutations. Explicit seeds can currently fill the whole pool ahead of structural/retrieval quotas; audit proposed, generated, legal, retained, actually attempted and eligible counts per region. Ensure an exploration slot reaches execution, not just a pool. Dense linear: on-chip vs staged/prequantized operands; backward: dX/dW ownership and split-workspace families; GLU/MLP: gate/down/recompute ownership and output stores crossed with dW splits; router: independent/global pools and row/column ownership; experts: preparation, compact vs scale-preserving execution, store and movement. Families own these meanings; generic code should not contain a growing list of family names.
3. **Use the cheapest scorer that changes a decision.** Start with an incumbent plus measured region coverage. Exact matched effects are soft: final summary materialized/fused dY = 1.045 across 27 compared expert invocations (27/27 >1%); packed row-major/expert-keys = 1.073 across 24, 19/24 >1%; packed native-warp/native-column QB = 0.842 across 24. These are conditional on other settings and sample selection, not hard pruning. Geometric retrieval returns *distinct execution regions* from compatible neighboring shapes; weight normalized distances in rows, capacity, reduction length and hardware ratios, not SKU/UUID. Keep a local control. Within well-supported regions try a depth-limited tree, then a small context-centered or pairwise ranker on **within-context log time ratios**, ignoring pairs within measured repeat noise. Flat first-hit comparison must include incumbent, coupled heuristic, retrieval-only, heuristic+retrieval, and same pool plus ranker. No global model needs to predict milliseconds to select a schedule.
4. **Separate optional heads and fallback.** An absolute head predicts log measured time minus a meaningful analytic log baseline; calibrate on eligible discovery anchors, test against context medians/retrieval, mask missing denominators and use explicit missingness. Useful for budgeting, not prerequisite to choose an order. Preparation head predicts cold/warm median/p90 wait separately from execution; current `compile_ms` includes setup and sometimes lease waits, and successful-only fits omit long failures. Learn compile success only from sufficient uncensored data, calibrated on held-out regions; never soft-pass a correctness failure or use a learned probability for legality. Out-of-support regime/architecture, missing profile, semantic mismatch, rank disagreement or too little held-out evidence → ordinary qualified opener + exploration. Ensemble spread is a warning; calibrate hit-within-2% and regret by support bucket. Extend [FamilyPriorPolicy/StagedPriorPolicy](../rtx/autotune/prior_policy.py) to optionally route by supported *region* in effort, not a separate public selector. Current progressive path installs retrieval coverage and a lazy `FrozenPriorSearch` arm, not the research policy's full offline permutation.
5. **Spend actual budgets.** Example flat *experiment*, not a new preset: first 8 distinct attempts = 1 incumbent + 2 geometry contrasts + 2 retrieved distinct plans + 1 strongest prior/local proposal + 1 local refinement + 1 diverse exploration. With no compatible donors substitute legal region alternatives; with aliases backfill novel proposals. For strong families preserve their existing eight-prefix and explicit user reservations. Continue the **same journal**: next 8 = e.g. 2 unexplored contrasted regions + 2 additional diverse attempts + 4 adaptive/local attempts; next 16 = 3 diverse + 3 unresolved alternatives + 10 evidence-guided/local. Release an exhausted arm's quota; never exceed cap. Existing [progressive scheduler](../rtx/autotune/effort.py) and [UCB](../rtx/autotune/bandit.py) already support milestones, arm cost, continuation and local search. Use eligible within-context improvement per wall-second, not improvement from a numerically valid but explicitly unstable score (`AdaptiveBanditScheduler.reward` currently tests `successful`). Include proposal time. Failed admitted attempts count; rejected precompile aliases do not, but generation time does. Separate unique-attempt cap from repeat/finalist executions, charge both to wall budget, and report unfinished attempts/deadline; `max_seconds` is admission, not preemption.

All of this runs only on the cold host selector. Keep [selection cells](../rtx/_moe_selection.py), [routing binding](../rtx/_routing_autotune.py), existing user-owned `torch.compile`, and cached hot dispatch. No feature extraction from CUDA tensors in a hot launcher, data-dependent host sync per call, private per-op graph, profiler in production, or extra eager tensor preprocessing.

## Correctness is a contract, not a tuning axis

There are two different equalities. [RoutingEvaluator](../rtx/autotune/routing.py) checks the hard algorithm and metadata at **identical probability input** exactly, and checks soft transport/score gradients against FP64. A complete forward that changes a legal FP32 reduction order may present slightly different scores to a **discontinuous** top-k/repair decision. Different final IDs are not proof of a broken hard kernel; conversely a close soft tensor or matching capacity is not proof of equivalent routes or gradients. One conflicting edge can cascade through capacity repair. The E56 change is unexplained, not certified as an innocent tie.

Keep the existing strict full-router promotion lane for fixed-route equivalence (including backward-only, transport-preserving changes, repeated replay, stable metadata and explicit deterministic contracts). Numerical equivalence for *necessarily identical route IDs on all inputs* is not a defensible default assumption about arbitrary legal forward reductions; nor should that contract silently change. If the project wants a second contract, name/version and explicitly opt into it: verify prescribed affinity/normalization and each differentiable leaf against independent high-precision references with the chosen hard path frozen; on stable-margin decisions require exactness; inspect the **first divergent decision/round**, local score gap versus measured/reference error, hard assignment/tie-breaking on identical scores, capacities, uniqueness, inverse/weights, multi-input behavior and replay determinism. Include adversarial exact ties, large-margin cases, near ties, normal and changed feeds; report route churn and downstream output/gradient changes separately. A different algorithm, scaling/rounding recipe, partition, or wrong hard choice on the same scores is not “FP32 drift.” If training semantics require identical policy trajectories, leave even a numerically qualified alternative opt-in.

The old [strict forward probe](../benchmarks/qualify_flat_router_forward.py) failed on its **control** against a full-router FP64 scalar adjoint: absolute differences 2.83e−5 and 2.09e−5 on the two logged probes exceeded 1e−5 absolute plus 2e−4 relative tolerance. No candidate comparison passed that lane. Diagnose upstream precision/conditioning vs implementation (same hard path, intermediate rounding, scalar cancellation and leaf-wise magnitudes); independently predeclare a justified scalar error budget or fix the control before any promotion. Do not retune a tolerance to the speed winner. All 31 large cases passed the independent quantized expert FP64/all-leaf/replay/live-memory gates; that is a different test.

**Predeclare the decision.** Suggested engineering targets, not confidence bounds from these data: at eight actual attempts, reduce median flat whole-layer regret by at least half vs ordinary, against an independently remeasured best legal whole configuration from the union of all deeper arms; no protected-family whole regression above 2% beyond interleaved visit noise, and no correctness, allocation, cache or replay regression. Report each shape, median/worst (and p90 only with enough independent contexts), paired uncertainty, no-winner rate, time/trials to within 2% of the observed oracle, cold invocation wall seconds and compiler/prep seconds spent on failed, duplicate or never-competitive plans. Any unresolved gate or stronger result obtained only on the same seven flat contexts is research evidence, not promotion.
