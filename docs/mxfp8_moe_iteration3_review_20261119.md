# Iteration 3 review: protect the cheap router trial, then remove native work

Baseline: [45-context study](balanced_moe_challenge_20261119.md), [iteration 2](mxfp8_moe_iteration2_20261119.md), runtime `ebec8ac46b0ad3b10902194ef56ce698988b2d5e19e3a1b01dd06ce766fec58b`. This is a source and archived-evidence review, not a new timing run. All estimates below are experiment targets, not measured gains.

| Priority | Change | Where it can pay | Absolute opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Protect a staged, sharded packed-router trial | Cold selection on uneven lengths | **916 µs measured whole** between four and eight admissions on one pack. Aim to recover most at four; not native-only. |
| 2 | Run the fragment GLU and persistent dWs with the existing active prefix | Packed rows whose physical upper bound substantially exceeds the actual prefix | **52 µs measured** from the older coupled active template; aim for **another 15–55 µs** on packed-32. The extra gain is unmeasured. |
| 3 | Vectorize token-owned forward combine and backward dX | Long/wide layers, including single pools with no padding to skip | **357–358 µs** in the two baseline kernels on each of two long workloads; a 10–25% reduction would fund **~35–90 µs** before whole-layer interactions. |

### 1. Protect actual admissions, not just a nicer seed list

The [uneven-pack journal](../autotune_reports/balanced_moe_challenge_20261119/packed_uneven/tuning/varlen_routing_effort/) records initial / local Torch / local native / persistent at 1.394 / 1.891 / 1.387 / 1.441 ms. The **first continuation trial** was already in the portfolio: staged Sinkhorn, warp rows, forward/backward 32-way column sharding, native-warp QB, row-major columns and cache capacity 16; .581 ms on the tuner objective. Ordinary whole F+B was 2.094 → 1.178 ms; BF16 benefited too. No new router math is required.

Sol 1 owns `rtx/autotune/varlen_routing.py`, focused router tests, and, if needed, **only** an opt-in adapter hook in `rtx/autotune/effort.py`. The current progressive initial neighborhood consumes slots before structural candidates; persistent eligibility can also precede staged trials inside `packed_structural_seeds`. Admit an eligible staged-sharded family no later than the third actual unique trial (prefer second when cold compile time threatens the budget); keep initial and a complementary option. Derive proposals from cold lengths, number of pools, cache fit, and legal axes; do not recognize a device name or length tuple. Unrelated adapters and persistent/short-pool alternatives must remain unchanged. Version/invalidate the packed-router opening only. Test exact progressive 2/4/8 observation order, continuation, deduplication, deadline accounting, and frozen hard assignments. Do not touch affinity gates or add a hot length read.

Known case: T8192/D384/H128/E32/K4/S256, lengths 127+513+2001+767+4095+689. **New:** same T/model with 33+95+257+769+1703+5335, plus a new mostly short balanced pack and one-pool controls. Freeze experts/shared/affinity, compare independently selected four versus cumulative eight on each; report both MX and same-router checkpointed BF16.

### 2. Preserve winning owners when skipping the padded suffix

On packed 32×256, logical allocation C=2016 but only 1024 sequence-aligned rows/expert are needed by this fixture. The original selected MX is 1.296876 ms; older active MX 1.244590; compact checkpointed BF16 **1.191677** on HOME. WORK independently gives 1.343208 native vs 1.236922 compact BF16 (no active WORK measurement). Closing the remaining HOME gap from older active needs **52.9 µs**; do not promise it. Static packed expert trace: fragment GLU 44.8 µs; two persistent dWs 67.8+34.2 µs. Skipping ~(2016−1024)/2016 of those would be ~72 µs of their *static* cost at linear scaling; that is not incremental benefit over the already-trimmed legacy owners.

Sol 2 owns `rtx/kernels/mxfp8_moe_prepared.py`, `mxfp8_moe_persistent_dw.py` and focused tests; parent integrates `_moe_execution.py`, config legality, and tuner templates. Wire the existing group-rounded device int32 prefix through the byte-scale ABI: `Plan.gemm` currently discards fragment active-M parameters and returns early for persistent dW. Permit only validated unsplit, row-major TMA kernels. Forward: skip an entire CTA uniformly **before** participating in the pipeline; produce through the last row tile the downstream down GEMM can consume. No fragment epilogue owner, word store, or barrier may selectively exit. Persistent dW: keep descriptor strides and MMA K order physical, use the same clamped ceil-divided dynamic K tile count for producer, scale staging and **all** consumer groups. Preserve inactive neighbor-panel barrier arrivals, and ensure zero-prefix writes zero dW without a blocking wait. This is internal output-panel reuse, not permission for arbitrary persistent-wave M scheduling.

Keep the existing full-reduction-tail scrub for qHT/qPT through the final dW K tile, and ensure qdYT/qXT producers define every byte/scale those descriptors can read. Buffers shared by the typed arena may contain prior live values: poison scratch, exercise partial last tiles and changed prefix at the **same addresses**. No new alias or scratch saved into autograd; no arena-size or traffic reduction claim from predication alone. Preserve sequence-local 32-row groups, invalid/zero-weight assignments, BF16 casts, FP32 reductions, and the old static and old active templates.

Test frozen static winner, older active, active+fragment only, active+persistent only, active+both, and compact BF16. **New:** lengths [63,193,127,641] repeated eight times give T8192 with a different active/physical ratio. Include empty/one-token pools, one-pool no-skip, mismatched producer/consumer M/K tile sizes and both reuse axes. Check packed bytes/scales where defined, output/all gradients, changed weights and upstream. Any persistent leg that fails remains individually unavailable; do not weaken the existing guard wholesale.

### 3. Share inverse-route metadata across a vector of features

`RoutedMovement.kernel` maps each combine/dX lane to one scalar output element: every rank iteration indexes the inverse map, derives the physical slot, and reads one BF16 value. On T65536/D384 and T32768/D768/H512 the isolated expert trace contains 203+153 and 197+161 µs respectively for these BF16-source movement launches. This is not measured DRAM saturation. Coalescing and caches may already hide some redundant loads. T32768/D768/H512 is already a genuine 10.083-ms whole, 1.239× checkpointed BF16, 1.445× routed case.

Sol 3 owns a new `rtx/kernels/mxfp8_moe_token_reduce.py` and independent `tests/test_moe_token_reduce.py`; parent connects only the two movement kinds and owns common config/search. One warp/subwarp owns a token-feature stripe, shares slot/weight metadata across lanes, loads adjacent BF16 values in an alignment-guarded vector, and writes contiguous FP32 combine / BF16 dX. Preserve exact rank order, multiply/add contraction and output-cast boundary; never multiply the dX a second time. Use physical addressing `expert * storage_c + local` with logical inverse slots validated against `e*c`; invalid slots contribute zero. No atomics, extra workspace, or aliased-placeholder writes. Tunable bounded vector widths and stripe ownership, retained scalar fallback, no register/SKU recipes. Do not change generic gather/route-gradient behavior. Measure one changed leg at a time then both; baseline already has contiguous neighboring lanes, so source-level byte counts alone are not a result.

**New:** T24576/D640/H192/E48/K6/S320 (single and uneven pack); smaller T3072/D384/H128/E96/K3 to guard small-C overhead. Test shifted pointers, width tails, K=1/3/4/6/8, dummy slots, strided upstream at the public wrapper, and valid zero route weights. Compare both kernels bitwise to identical arithmetic and to an independent rank-ordered reference; match all optimizer-visible gradients through captured fullgraph replay.

### Integration and deferrals

Use fresh revisioned tuner evidence and protected *complete* compositions when legal. Root owns shared `_moe_execution.py`, `configs/moe*.py`, `autotune/moe.py`, fingerprints and common benchmark seams. Compare single changes and combined against the same frozen inputs, params, dY and hard-route hashes; four interleaved visits, seven ordinary-event samples, actual cold admissions; verify changed inputs/dY, independent quantized FP64 expert oracle, all leaves finite, unchanged live bytes and no new public allocation/stream requirements. Keep compact BF16 beside original BF16 on packed tests. Do not add isolated phase deltas to predict whole speedup.

Do not spend this wave doubling generic FFN trials: four 8→16 controls did not materially improve deployment. Packed input/dY/XT preparation still traverses physical storage (packed-32 dY 39.5, indexed XT 28.2 µs); prioritize that next if active-compute composition leaves the compact BF16 gap. Small-T shared is a separate unresolved loss (tiny shared .1130 ms vs .0185 ms checkpointed BF16); retest complete small-M shared families before claiming these three changes fix it. Do not interpret graph-node omissions as Python gaps: Systems recorded one graph launch, no individual launches and 202/177 kernels versus Torch 163/163 on normal/outlier inputs.

Finally, numerical fidelity to the MX recipe is not training fidelity. Worst independent expert NRMS .0274%; BF16 output NRMS 6.32–6.88%, dX 6.44–6.59%, worst matrix-gradient 8.13%. Long scalar score-bias differed by .01253 vs .01407 reference magnitude (89.1%); cancellation and training impact are unproved. Preserve the five rejected affinity candidates and existing gate; use an independent high-precision affinity oracle and multi-seed real-loss scalar checks separately.
