# MoE iteration 4 review: stop preparing unused rows; give small shared forwards a fair trial

Review only. No new GPU runs, tests, or profiling. The recommendations below use the [iteration-3 results and raw journals](../autotune_reports/mxfp8_moe_iteration3_20261119/summary.json), the [45-context study](balanced_moe_challenge_20261119.md), and the current source. Scope: warmed fixed-buffer synthetic training forward + backward, HOME RTX 5090 GPU0, not convergence. End-of-iteration runtime digest `8f1003f6a34e9b06352b47ecd053adebfcaefddbb9c7e6dcc42bb3074eed3b2e`; MoE revision 14, portfolio 25, router semantic/opening 7/1.

Keep descriptors and winning GEMMs. | Four producers cost **94.21 µs** in the archived static packed-32 expert trace. The first two bets have identifiable kernel costs and independent landing paths. The third attacks something a suffix predicate cannot fix; it has the most architectural risk. None establishes a route to 1.2× by itself.

Times are whole-layer ms. Parity means recovering MX minus compact BF16; 1.2× means reaching compact BF16 / 1.2, at fixed BF16 time. Native/compacted comparison as BF16 ÷ native is **0.992× / 0.941×**. Native beats the original checkpoint reference, not the stronger one. Every named whole-layer output/gradient has zero measured difference across MX schedules on both fixtures; live bytes were flat. The quantized FP64 expert oracle tests implementation fidelity, not MX parity with unquantized BF16 or real-loss behavior.

**Why two packs differ.** [The router](../rtx/kernels/routing_varlen.py) rounds each length up to a multiple of E/gcd(E,K) before balancing, then pads each per-expert quota to a 32-row group. [The expert prefix](../rtx/kernels/mxfp8_moe_active.py) finds one global maximum local slot over the real inverse assignments, rounds to 32, and changes M/K work bounds, **not** storage or descriptor pitch. For these E/K, before scale padding the per-pool quota is ceil(length/8):

| Rows/expert | 32 × 256 | Irregular × 8 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean real load (T×K/E); scheduled quota including dummy assignments | 1024; 1024 | 1024; (8+25+16+81)×8 = 1040 |
| Extent after independent group padding | 32×32 = 1024 | (32+32+32+96)×8 = 1536 |
| Same shape-only native capacity; suffix beyond group extent | 2016; 992 (49.2%) | 2016; 480 (23.8%) |

The 496 rows between 1040 and 1536 are **inside** the irregular group-padded span. Even its partially filled 32-row groups contain real values. Neither a maximum-slot predicate nor an all-empty-group test removes those groups. At K-tile 128, the two active spans occupy 8 vs 12 tiles, versus 16 static. The [BF16 control](../benchmarks/benchmark_moe_checkpoint.py) has no leading-axis scale contract: its differentiable valid-row remap uses a 1052 shape-only bound rounded to 1152 on **both** packs. Its gather/remap and checkpoint recompute are timed; neither its storage bound nor its 433 µs advantage over the original BF16 layout is a native speed forecast.

Whole prefix-only savings were **77.31 vs 4.55 µs**; adding token legs saved another **13.91 vs 13.92 µs**. Less skippable suffix, unchanged full-capacity preparation, tile rounding and prefix setup are credible mechanisms, **not** a quantitative attribution of that 17-fold difference. The isolated packed-32 expert F+B went 533.37 → 443.92 (older active owners) → 434.58 (+fragment) → 414.55 µs (+both persistent dWs). On irregular, the fresh 8-trial expert objectives began 532.08 → 493.25 → 481.75 µs; these are not the whole timing harness. Neither the irregular whole result nor its changing router workload can be reconstructed by adding isolated times. There is no matched irregular per-phase trace here.

Do not reinvest in the last two wins as if their original headroom were still available: paired long/wide movement graphs saved just ~5–6 µs, the two K6 controls slightly lost, whereas packed-32 and small-K3 paired movement improved 24.57→11.00 and 9.98→4.87 µs. Keep scalar and independent per-leg choices. The [router opening](mxfp8_moe_iteration3_router_20261119.md) moved staged sharding to actual admission 2: four-trial whole 2.089→1.176 and 2.557→1.431 ms, balanced .658 unchanged; eight-trial winners unchanged. Both precisions share that benefit. Freeze the router when testing native work.

## 1. Sol A — skip physical-capacity preparation without changing layout

**Source.** In [Builder.forward_operands / joint_backward](../rtx/_moe_execution.py), the incumbent quantizes X globally once per phase. `PackedInputGather` copies row bytes/scales into every physical slot in **both** phases; backward also emits the validated int32 token map. `RoutedDYQuant` reads FP32 dY and route weight, rounds their product to BF16, emits independent row dY for down-dX and leading-axis dYᵀ for down-dW. `IndexedExpertTranspose` uses the map to quantize **original BF16 X**, not decoded row-FP8 X, for gate-dW. Both process the physical slot extent. Global X/GU/down preparation does not shrink with the suffix.

In the [full ordered packed-32 trace](../autotune_reports/balanced_moe_challenge_20261119/packed_32/result.expert_step.trace.json) the two gathers cost 12.608+13.888, dY 39.488, Xᵀ 28.224 µs. Multiplying their sum by suffix fraction assumes uniform cost; invalid rows already suppress source loads. The five D-wide packed outputs (two gathered X, both dY orientations, Xᵀ) model **62.85 / 30.41 MB** of avoidable suffix byte+scale writes before consumer rounding, not measured DRAM transfers or allocator savings.

**Implementation.** Own [packed gather](../rtx/kernels/mxfp8_moe_input.py), [routed dY](../rtx/kernels/mxfp8_moe_dy.py), [indexed transpose](../rtx/kernels/mxfp8_moe_warp_quant.py), focused producer tests. Add an optional bounded-work mode: per-expert warp/CTA ownership for gather; make both rasters of each quantizer enumerate only the necessary tiles with device bounds. Keep grid/descriptor strides static, map all invalid slots to −1 within the readable extent, and define neutral FP8 **and** all-zero E8M0 bytes there. No return by a subset of lanes across barriers. Start with the selected indexed/fused-dY, dual-orientation-weight, row-major TMA composition; separate/fused-all or mma128 modes stay untouched until individually qualified.

For each output derive the readable envelope from **all** consuming M tiles, recompute alignment and final dW K tiles, clamped to storage. Existing tail initialization covers qHᵀ/qPᵀ only; it does not protect stale qXᵀ/q(dY)ᵀ or their map. The persistent down/gate dW producers, scale loaders and every consumer team must agree on the final K iteration, including zero-prefix zero dW. Preserve the valid zero-weight route adjoint, original public dweights shape and zero invalid slots. Keep the old full producer to isolate each leg. Report issued work/bytes separately from static arena size (historical packed-32 backward arena model **217.1 MB**); predication alone does not shrink it.

## 2. Sol B — fix a demonstrated small-T shared search hole before writing a new GEMM

At T128/D384/shared H256, isolated shared F+B is 112.96 vs 18.46 µs checkpointed BF16; whole MX/BF16 is 475.26/342.25 µs. The [actual 8→16 shared journal](../autotune_reports/balanced_moe_challenge_20261119/tiny_128/shared-tuning/d7f5793f76afbec4a818a7d9/0c99f34ac4fa0cddbd3daa6a/observations.jsonl) records **onchip/onchip forward in every observation**, despite varying backward. The winning forward's GLU (34.303) and mixed down (33.376 µs) are identified by their operand shapes in the [whole trace](../autotune_reports/balanced_moe_challenge_20261119/tiny_128/result.mxfp8_step.trace.json). At T512 the selected forward is prequant/prequant (55.97/26.77 µs shared F+B); at T2048 the deficit is 7.86 µs. A shared-only change cannot explain the whole tiny-case gap or solve packed-32.

**First deliverable:** in [mlp_structural_seeds / make_mlp_tuner](../rtx/autotune/mlp.py) protect an actually legal prepared forward on the **same** winning/compatible joint-backward policy; build unsplit direct dWs before spending trials on 4/8-way dWs with too few nonempty K panels. Compare gate-only, then gate+down prequant against the paired on-chip control. Derive legality from M/N/K, transpose extents and available reduction tiles, not an SKU or T table. The missing admission is measured; a prepared win is not. If it wins, ship coverage first. If separate preparation is still costly, test a single tiled X/GU/down dynamic-weight preparation launch feeding the two prepared GEMMs, holding backward fixed. Do not promise all 67.68 µs is removable.

Own the ordinary-MLP tuner, a new private compiler-visible forward-plan wrapper/kernel and MLP tests as needed. [BalancedMoE.forward](../rtx/moe.py) calls the ordinary MLP on **all T tokens**, once per shared expert, adds its BF16 output to routed FP32, then casts. [selected_shared](../rtx/_moe_shared.py) binds cold selection, then dispatches the static MLP. [mlp.py](../rtx/mlp.py) saves X/GU/down only; `_backward` performs down-dX → joint derivative/hidden recompute → down-dW and gate dX/dW, with separate partial-grad paths. Do not optimize a detached benchmark or pad shared M to expert capacity. Legacy `_mlp_op/_run_forward` allocates internally: do **not** copy that pattern for the new prepared arm. Use the same visible output/typed-scratch allocation and launch-only mutation boundary for forced, bound and candidate execution; preserve cold token/replay, partial gradients, and no weight cache. Keep the existing small/long/half-aligned policies as controls.

## 3. Sol C — separate compact forward M from sequence-local backward K

Start with a **forward-only private row layout** on the selected fragment/direct-down path. Use a native device map from original slots to stable dense rows and back: either use sequence scheduled/physical offsets and keep scheduled dummy slots, or rank inverse-validated real slots per expert. Start with one, e.g. the scheduled-offset map; compare including map construction. The router already calculates the two offset vectors, but `_dispatch_experts` receives ids/weights/inverse, **not** the device offsets. Supply private metadata on the packed module path or construct it from device boundaries outside the router's public ABI; do not guess boundaries from ids or a cold histogram on replay. Reject the compact arm for opaque routes without proven bounds. Use the safe shape-only quota bound plus required kernel alignment; execute only through the current device extent. Public `expert*C+local` addressing and hard assignments stay unchanged; `compact_stride` is a separate internal value.

Gather token-row quantized X directly to compact storage; fragment GLU and down have feature-axis reductions, so a stable M remap does not change their 32-value scale membership. Combine through a translated inverse in original rank order, not an expanded BF16 expert output. Backward can initially recompute from the **unchanged original** route/layout at the same addresses. Measure a forward-only mode *and its complete F+B*: the archived 78.88 µs gate+down pair is a static different-pack ceiling on the comparable work, **not** an irregular timing. A toy linear rescaling of that pair by (1536−1040)/2016 gives ~19 µs before mapping, tile rounding and interaction. On 32×256 there are no internal holes to win over active M; require a control there. The compact path can reduce the planner's actual forward storage: e.g. one [32,C,384] BF16 result at C=2016→1152 is **21.23 MB smaller**, before map cost/lifetime; this is not a peak- or bandwidth measurement.

Do **not** pack all dW inputs contiguously and reuse their row scales. qXᵀ/q(dY)ᵀ/qHᵀ/qPᵀ each needs the original sequence-local group membership, including zeros in partial groups; persistent dWs consume them along token K. Exchanging two partial groups for a single dense 32-value block changes amax, scale, bytes and possibly every affected gradient. Dropping whole all-zero groups is not the same as merging partial groups. A later mixed backward may run M-only dX/route work densely but must reconstruct original-group tiles before computing any axis-dependent scale (qHᵀ from decoded hidden, qPᵀ from BF16-rounded derivative), preserve K/reduction order and tail scrubs, and translate routing-weight adjoints back to [E,C]. Unless that scatter/requant cost pays in whole F+B, leave backward on the old layout. Treat any cross-sequence reblocking as a **different numerical policy**, out of scope.

Own a new private compact-metadata/layout kernel, compact gather/combine adapters and focused layout tests. Keep the forward adapter independent of Sol A's bounded original-layout producers. Integration owns common [Builder/allocator](../rtx/_moe_execution.py), `_packed_expert_spec/_dispatch_experts`, config fields/rejection, evaluator plumbing and fingerprints. No hand-tuned register caps, forced BF16 fallback, eager tensor arithmetic inside launch ops, materialized cat/permute, device readback, new caller workspace/stream API, or autograd-saved transient buffers. Preserve virtual packed weight/leaf identities and native MXFP8 MMA, FP32 activation/route reductions, BF16 module boundaries.
