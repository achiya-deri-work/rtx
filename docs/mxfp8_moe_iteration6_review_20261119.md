**Decision:** Give the packed expert's joint recompute and the flat router's *forward* column normalization one matched structural competition each. Give the third owner the capture-lifetime failure, not another shared-MLP sweep. The first two competitions can ship as search-only improvements. Neither requires a new kernel to test. A negative comparison or an unreproduced fault is a valid stopping point.

Start from runtime `759c030b5867e029ebdd40def2bac8d8ebee82154075d06a2a79c31b324a79b9` and the **explicit improved cells** in the [iteration 6 context](mxfp8_moe_iteration6_context_20261119.md). ## 1. Packed experts: change the joint recompute tile, not the backward scale groups

**Owner A:** [expert tuner](../rtx/autotune/moe.py), dedicated test/runner/report only. Both improved packed winners use `dual_operand`, 64×64×128, one stage, vector-4/XOR. The [matched frozen expert steps](mxfp8_moe_iteration5_experts_20261119.md) cost 1.732/2.502 ms (D384/D512); no isolated time for *this* recompute is known. It produces four different packed operands from the repeated gate/up projection. N=2H is 384/512: doubling N tiles from 64 to 128 in width changes the number of column tiles per M tile from 6/8 to 3/4. This can reduce repeated activation-panel loads and tile setup, but doubles output width per CTA and can increase staging or lower parallelism. It does not halve arithmetic or establish a traffic bottleneck.

**First experiment:** time the selected joint kernel on its actual populated original-row, bounded backward buffers, plus complete compiled frozen-route expert F+B. Compare just three generated plans: selected 64×64/1 dual, 64×128/2 dual, 64×128/1 operand reuse. A CPU-only check of `joint_recompute_seeds` against the actual archived winner and shape sidecars verified the incumbent is emitted **exactly** and both alternatives are legal at D384 and D512. If needed the generator also emits 64×64/1 operand reuse to distinguish engine from width. Start at D384; assess D512 before promoting. Keep the compact forward, active prefix, producer bounds, both persistent dWs, both independent quantized orientations, precision and row identities unchanged. No forward activations saved; no dense backward K regrouping; no resurrection of the slower split-warp engine.

Only if a candidate wins component and whole F+B, expose one legal alternative on the bounded+compact anchor in the eight-trial opening. Derive it from the workload, scratch legality, N-grid and existing seed family, not a register cap or per-SKU table. Retain the improved family and exploration; show exactly which admission was displaced and test its control. Stop if the honest per-kernel effect is negligible or a kernel win disappears in the whole step; do not spend another wave cycling knobs. Unseen packed admission: **B8×1216, D512/E128/K6/H256/S512**, active width exactly 4D. Verify forward/dX/all weights and real/dummy route adjoints against the independent operational FP64 oracle, changed dY/boundaries, padding poison and stable memory.

## 2. Flat router: compare forward column geometry with the successful backward fixed

**Owner B:** [flat routing tuner](../rtx/autotune/routing.py), dedicated test/runner/report only. Do not touch packed routing or the shared fixture. The [flat geometry comparison](mxfp8_moe_iteration5_router_20261119.md) measured the improved E32 full router at 2.7205 ms, saved Sinkhorn backward at 0.2481 ms and its final column adjoint at 40.95 µs. Those are nested standalone timings, **not** the cost of forward. Its forward remains 128 threads, one row group, four columns/CTA: 8 column CTAs, 512 elements per scan lane at T16384. The [forward kernel](../rtx/kernels/routing_sinkhorn.py) scans for max, exp-sum and history; backward's measured geometry win establishes no forward speedup.

**First experiment:** on `flat-bc.json:combined`, hold hard QB/quota/metadata, FP32 algorithm, affinity, solver iterations and the selected backward 256/1/1 fixed. Time one real column normalization on a populated row shift, full Sinkhorn forward including history, and full router F+B. Compare original forward 128/1/4 against 128/1/1 and 256/1/1; these separate group count from block size. The thread change also affects row kernels; measure the full forward. Use the E128 winner (backward 256/1/2) as a second geometry control if E32 pays. Do not substitute a packed pool, invent theoretical occupancy, or pursue a sharded adjoint in this iteration.

If a complete-path win survives, offer a forward geometry **on top of** the measured backward opening in the existing budget. Derive group/threads from actual forward scan length, E and SM grid; preserve early hard and backward controls, explicit priors and deduplication. Unseen flat admission: **T14336, D384/E56/K4/H320/S256** (B8×1792 diagnostic partition; one global pool), active width exactly 4D. Require exact hard IDs/inverse on the **actual selected forward's** scores versus control, including changed-input replay; checking hard selection twice on common oracle probabilities alone cannot detect a crossed quota/tie. Retain existing FP64 transport, soft weight and score-adjoint gates, every router gradient including near-zero scalar adjoints, and fresh-vs-replay verification. If hard IDs change on a target, reject that arm for the matched comparison; don't relax gates to admit it. Stop if no stable absolute whole-step win; remeasure compact checkpoint and saved BF16 with the identical selected router. This is shared-router time, not an MXFP8-only gain.

## 3. Graph lifetime: turn an intermittent failure into a controlled regression

**Owner C:** [graph resource tracking](../rtx/_resources.py), dedicated resource-lifetime tests/diagnostic/report only. No tuner, ML kernel, general cache-policy or shared benchmark changes. The original packed factorial failed at the third capture's `capture_end`; C-only passed, and the same factorial passed with benchmark-only cyclic-GC isolation. [Iteration 5](mxfp8_moe_iteration5_20261119.md) records a nonsticky failure, not its origin. Inspection shows `_release_graph` synchronizes the owning device when pins exist, catches exceptions, then clears the dictionary. Neither that call nor an LRU eviction is established as the cause of the historic incident.

**First experiment:** in short fresh processes, warm a real RTX runner and capture a graph with verified nonempty pins. Arrange controlled destruction of its last reference during a *different* graph's capture on the same GPU; distinguish weak finalization from explicit reset. Compare the identical drop outside capture and a no-drop control. Record finalizer entry/exit, capture state, exact first CUDA error/sync attempt and pin counts without adding a synchronizing probe. Distinguish the RTX pin finalizer from conditional routing graph and hard-plan destructors; Torch 2.13's graph-entry collection depends on `force_cudagraph_gc`, not every graph entrance. Separate instrumentation from uninstrumented confirmation. A forced finalizer repro proves that path is unsafe, **not** that it caused the original failure; correlating the original sequence requires its own evidence.

Ship a lifetime fix only with repeatable failure of the old path and success of the new one. If proved, retain the owners until prior asynchronous replay is safe and release at a bounded safe boundary outside capture; protect two independently live graphs, reset, exception exits and changing streams. Simply catching a sync error, checking only one unrelated stream, or dropping pins early is not a fix. No permanent GC disable, cache enlargement, host sync in replay, or caller-owned cleanup API. Repeated capture/drop cycles must plateau in live bytes/pending owners and not leak after teardown. If nondeterministic, ship the bounded diagnostic/evidence alone and leave production unchanged.

For an actual fix, repeat the packed multi-cell sequence with default GC, exact every-grad and changed-data replay, then run one genuine existing eight-trial cold search on a fresh in-budget packed fixture (e.g. **B8×1088 D384/E128/K6/H192/S384**, exact 4D) through warm/capture/release. No new search axis belongs to lifetime management. Preserve selected-plan eligibility and whole improved-baseline replay; acceptance is reliable capture/retirement with no material timing regression, **not** a claimed speedup. Use a tiny-forward live-graph control.
